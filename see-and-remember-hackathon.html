The flagships — pick one

Each flagship is one experiment that makes a chapter's central claim visible. The notebook already runs it; your job is to push it and explain it.

CNN track · stations 1–4, 6

Flagship A — Dense vs Conv Showdown

A convolutional net matches a dense net on images while using far fewer parameters and losing less accuracy when the picture shifts — because connections are local, weights are shared, and pooling tolerates small moves.

Train on Fashion-MNIST. Reach the accuracy target with a small CNN, then compare it head-to-head with a dense network on two axes the chapter cares about: parameter count and translation robustness.

Deliverables

  1. CNN test accuracy ≥ 90%.
  2. Parameter count of the CNN vs the dense net (expect roughly a 10× gap).
  3. Shift-robustness: each model's accuracy when every test image is rolled a few pixels, and the drop.

Winning insight: the CNN's smaller accuracy drop under shift is the equivariance-plus-pooling story of stations 3–4, made measurable.

RNN track · stations 2, 3, 6, 7, 8

Flagship B — Memory Showdown

A plain RNN forgets across long gaps (the vanishing gradient), while a gated cell carries information across them through its additive memory path.

Use the chapter's recall task: show a signal token at the first step, then a run of blanks, and require the network to reproduce the first token at the last step. The gap is the sequence length. Race a plain RNN against an LSTM as the gap grows.

Deliverables

  1. Accuracy-vs-gap plot for a plain RNN and an LSTM on the same task.
  2. The gap where the plain RNN slides toward chance (1⁄8) while the LSTM holds.
  3. One sentence linking the RNN's collapse to the product of Jacobians in BPTT.

Winning insight: the LSTM's flat line is the constant error carousel of station 7 — additive memory keeps the gradient alive where multiplication kills it.

+15Gradient-norm evidence

Concept track (no training required)

For mixed teams or anyone who'd rather reason than code. Two deliverables, judged together for up to 60 points: answer the boss-battle below, and build one original visualization or a one-page explainer of a concept you choose, using the chapters' live labs as reference.

  1. Why does a fully-connected layer scale badly on images, and which three CNN properties fix it?
  2. What does a single value in a feature map measure? What is a kernel?
  3. Name the three stages of a convolutional layer. Why does pooling give approximate translation invariance?
  4. Relate V1 simple and complex cells to convolution and pooling. What is a Gabor filter?
  5. “Convolution is a dense layer with an infinitely strong prior.” State the two constraints, and what pooling's prior asserts.
  6. Define stride, padding, and dilation, and give the output-size formula.
  7. What is a structured output? How does convolution's spatial preservation enable segmentation?
  8. Write the vanilla RNN cell equations. What does the hidden state represent, and what is parameter sharing across time?
  9. In BPTT, why is a shared weight's gradient a sum over steps, and where does the product of Jacobians appear?
  10. List the five RNN input–output types with one task each.
  11. Why can't a bidirectional RNN be used for streaming / causal prediction?
  12. Describe the LSTM's three gates and the additive cell update. Why is it a constant error carousel, and how does the GRU differ?
